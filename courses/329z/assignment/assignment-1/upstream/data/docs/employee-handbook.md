# Cardinal Energy Employee Handbook

Version 4.2
Effective: 2025-09-01
Owner: People Operations (Tessa Nguyen, Director of People Operations)
Approved by: Marcus Delgado, VP People

This handbook applies to all employees of Cardinal Energy, Inc. in the United States. Employees in the Rotterdam office follow this handbook except where the Netherlands addendum, published on Perch, says otherwise.

## 1. Welcome

Welcome to Cardinal Energy. We build software and devices that help electric utilities understand what is happening on their distribution grids. Our customers are investor-owned utilities, municipal utilities, and rural cooperatives. They use our GridLens software, our Meridian M4 meter gateways, and our Pulse S2 line sensors to forecast load, find outages, and measure losses.

Cardinal Energy was founded in 2016 in Oakland, California. Our headquarters is at 1450 Harborline Street in Oakland. Our hardware lab is in Boulder, Colorado, and we opened an office in Rotterdam in 2024 to serve European customers.

This handbook describes how we work, what you can expect from the company, and what the company expects from you. It is a summary. Several topics have their own detailed policy document, and the handbook names that document where one exists. Section 36 explains what to do when the handbook and another document disagree.

## 2. Mission and values

Our mission is to give every utility a clear, current picture of its grid. We hold four values.

Measure first. We make decisions from data, and we say so when we do not have the data.

Own the outcome. The person who starts a piece of work follows it until a customer can use it.

Be direct and kind. We give feedback early, in plain words, and with respect.

Keep the lights on. Our customers run critical infrastructure. Reliability and safety come before speed.

## 3. How to use this handbook

Read the whole handbook during your first two weeks. After that, use it as a reference. The numbered sections are stable between versions so that links from Perch keep working. If you have a question that the handbook does not answer, ask your manager first and People Operations second. You can reach People Operations at people@cardinal-energy.example or through the People queue in the HelpDesk portal.

The handbook is not a contract of employment. Nothing in it changes the at-will nature of employment described in Section 4.

## 4. Employment basics

Employment at Cardinal Energy in the United States is at will. Either you or the company may end the employment relationship at any time, with or without cause or notice, subject to applicable law. Only the CEO may make an agreement that changes this, and only in writing.

Cardinal Energy is an equal opportunity employer. We do not discriminate on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, national origin, age, disability, veteran status, or any other status protected by law. This applies to hiring, pay, promotion, assignment, training, and separation.

We provide reasonable accommodation to qualified employees with disabilities and for sincerely held religious practices. To request an accommodation, contact People Operations. Requests are confidential.

Employees are classified as exempt or non-exempt under wage and hour law, and as full-time (30 or more scheduled hours per week) or part-time. Your offer letter states your classification. Non-exempt employees record their hours in PeopleHub each week and must have manager approval before working overtime.

## 5. Introductory period

The first 90 days of employment are an introductory period. During this time you and your manager agree on a 30-60-90 day plan, and your manager meets with you at least every two weeks to review progress. Your manager completes a short written check-in at the end of the introductory period. Benefits start on your first day and do not depend on completing the introductory period. The introductory period does not change the at-will relationship.

## 6. Working hours

Most teams work a standard week of 40 hours. Core hours are 10:00 to 15:00 in the local time of your assigned office. Schedule meetings that involve more than one team inside core hours when you can. Outside core hours you set your own schedule with your manager.

Non-exempt employees receive a paid 10-minute rest break for every four hours worked and an unpaid 30-minute meal break for shifts longer than five hours.

Overtime for non-exempt employees is paid at one and a half times the regular hourly rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a week, or as state law requires where it is more generous. Time spent traveling for work during your normal working hours counts as hours worked. Record all hours accurately. Working without recording the time is not permitted, even if you offer to do so.

If you expect to be late or absent, tell your manager before your normal start time. An absence of three consecutive working days without notice is treated as a resignation unless there was an emergency that prevented you from making contact.

Teams with an on-call rotation follow the On-Call and Incident Process for anything that concerns on-call work, including pay for on-call weeks.

## 7. Hybrid and remote work

Each role is classified as hybrid, remote, or on-site. Your offer letter states your classification. Hybrid employees live within 50 miles of a Cardinal office. As of the effective date of this handbook, hybrid employees work from their assigned office at least two days each week, and the anchor days are Tuesday and Thursday. Remote employees work from home and travel to an office for quarterly team weeks. On-site roles, such as lab technicians and Workplace staff, work from the office or lab every working day.

The Remote and Hybrid Work Policy has the full rules, including the home office stipend and the rules for working temporarily from another location.

## 8. Compensation and payroll

Salaries are disbursed semi-monthly, on the 15th and on the last business day of each month. If the 15th falls on a weekend or a bank holiday, the deposit is made on the business day before. Pay statements are available in PeopleHub. Report any error in your pay statement to payroll@cardinal-energy.example within 30 days.

Cardinal Energy sets pay ranges for each job level using market data that the People team refreshes each year. Ranges are published on Perch. Your manager can tell you where your salary falls in the range for your level.

Compensation is reviewed once a year. Changes from the annual review take effect on April 1. An employee who joined after October 1 of the previous year is normally reviewed in the following cycle.

Sales and Customer Success roles may have a variable compensation plan. The plan document for your role governs how variable pay is earned and paid.

## 9. Performance and growth

We hold performance reviews twice a year, in January and in July. Each review has a self-assessment, peer feedback from three to five colleagues, and a written review from your manager. The January review is the input to the annual compensation review. The July review is a check-in on goals and growth and does not change pay.

Promotions are considered in both cycles. Your manager writes the promotion case, and a panel of leaders from your organization reviews it against the published level guide for your job family.

If your manager has concerns about your performance, they will tell you directly and in writing, agree on specific goals with you, and set a date to review them.

Between reviews, you and your manager hold a one-on-one meeting at least every two weeks. The meeting is yours: bring the topics you want to discuss, including workload, feedback, and career plans. Teams set goals each quarter and record them in PeopleHub, where anyone in the company can read them. Your individual goals should connect to your team's goals, and you and your manager revisit them at the start of each quarter.

Feedback is not limited to review cycles. If a colleague's work helped you, tell them and tell their manager. If something is not working, raise it with the person first, soon after it happens, and describe the effect it had and what you would like instead.

## 10. Equity

Most full-time employees receive stock options when they join. Options vest over four years with a one-year cliff: 25 percent vests on your first anniversary, and the rest vests monthly over the following 36 months. Your grant agreement and the equity plan document govern your options. The Finance team holds an equity information session each quarter. Cardinal Energy is a private company, and the handbook cannot give tax advice. Talk to a tax adviser before you exercise options.

## 11. Benefits overview

The company offers medical, dental, and vision insurance, a 401(k) plan with a company match, life and disability insurance, a wellness allowance, a professional development allowance, a commuter benefit, and an employee assistance program. Coverage begins on your first day. You have 30 days from your start date to enroll. After that, you can change your elections only during open enrollment each November or after a qualifying life event such as marriage or the birth of a child.

The Benefits Summary for the current plan year lists the plans, the amounts, and the company contributions. It is the authority on benefit amounts. This handbook does not repeat them.

## 12. Paid time off

Full-time employees accrue paid time off (PTO) each pay period. The rate depends on your length of service: 18 days per year during your first two years, 22 days per year from your second anniversary, and 25 days per year from your fifth anniversary. Part-time employees accrue in proportion to their scheduled hours.

As of the effective date of this handbook, you may carry over up to 5 days (40 hours) of unused PTO into the next calendar year, and carried-over days must be used by March 31. PTO above the carryover limit is forfeited on December 31, so plan your time off during the year.

Request PTO in PeopleHub. Requests for three or more consecutive days need two weeks of notice. The PTO Policy has the full rules.

## 13. Holidays

Cardinal Energy observes 11 paid company holidays each year in the United States: New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Presidents' Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day, the day after Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, and Christmas Day. When a holiday falls on a Saturday, it is observed on the Friday before. When it falls on a Sunday, it is observed on the Monday after.

Each employee also has two floating holidays per calendar year for days of personal, cultural, or religious significance. Floating holidays do not carry over.

Our offices close for the winter closure from December 24 through January 1. The working days in the closure that are not company holidays are paid and are not deducted from your PTO balance. Employees who must work during the closure, for example on-call engineers, receive equivalent days off in January.

## 14. Sick leave

Full-time employees receive 10 days of paid sick leave per calendar year, available from the first day of the year. Use sick leave for your own illness, injury, or medical appointments, or to care for a family member. Sick leave does not carry over and is not paid out at separation. If you are out sick for more than three consecutive working days, People Operations may ask for a note from a health care provider. If you are sick, stay home. Do not come to the office with a contagious illness.

## 15. Parental and family leave

Cardinal Energy provides paid parental leave to employees who become parents through birth, adoption, or foster placement. As of the effective date of this handbook, birthing parents receive 16 weeks of leave at full base pay and non-birthing parents receive 12 weeks at full base pay. Employees are eligible after six months of employment. The Parental Leave Policy has the full rules, including notice and the phased return to work.

Employees may also be eligible for unpaid, job-protected family and medical leave under federal and state law. People Operations will explain your options. Contact them as early as you can.

## 16. Bereavement leave

Employees receive up to 5 days of paid bereavement leave for the death of an immediate family member: a spouse or partner, child, parent, sibling, grandparent, or grandchild, including step and in-law relationships. Employees receive up to 2 days for the death of an extended family member or a close friend. If you need more time, talk to your manager about PTO or unpaid leave. Tell your manager as soon as you can. You do not need to provide documentation.

## 17. Jury duty, court appearances, and voting

If you are called for jury duty, tell your manager and send a copy of the summons to People Operations. Cardinal Energy pays your full salary for up to 10 working days of jury service per calendar year. If a trial runs longer, talk to People Operations. You keep any juror fees paid by the court.

If you are subpoenaed as a witness in a matter that is not related to the company, use PTO or a floating holiday.

If your work schedule does not leave you enough time to vote in a public election, you may take up to 2 hours of paid time at the start or end of your working day. Tell your manager at least two working days ahead.

## 18. Volunteer time

Each full-time employee may take up to 16 hours of paid volunteer time per calendar year to work with a registered nonprofit organization or a public school. Record volunteer time in PeopleHub under the Volunteer category and get your manager's approval before the day. Volunteer time does not carry over. Political campaign work does not qualify.

## 19. Sabbatical

After 5 years of continuous service, a full-time employee is eligible for a paid sabbatical of 4 weeks. You become eligible again after each further five years of service. Agree on the dates with your manager at least three months ahead. A sabbatical must be taken as one continuous block and within 18 months of becoming eligible. You may add up to one week of PTO to a sabbatical. Sabbatical time has no cash value and is not paid out at separation.

## 20. Travel and expenses

Cardinal Energy reimburses reasonable business expenses. Book travel through TripDesk and submit expenses in Ledgerly with receipts. The Travel and Expense Policy in force on the date you incur an expense governs the amounts. As of the effective date of this handbook, the meal allowance for domestic travel is $60 per day, and any single expense over $500 needs approval from your department's vice president in addition to your manager.

Do not use personal funds for purchases that should go through Procurement, such as software subscriptions, lab equipment, and anything that requires a contract. Send those requests to procurement@cardinal-energy.example.

## 21. Equipment and IT

IT issues each employee a laptop and any accessories that the role requires. Equipment belongs to the company and must be returned when you leave. Keep your laptop with you or locked away when you travel. If your laptop is lost or stolen, follow the device reporting procedure in the IT and Security Policy immediately. Do not wait until the next working day.

Request hardware, software, and access through the HelpDesk portal at help.cardinal-energy.example. The IT HelpDesk is staffed Monday to Friday. Install only software from the approved list unless IT has approved an exception.

Company systems are provided for work. Limited personal use is acceptable if it does not interfere with your work, cost the company money, or break any policy. The company may monitor its systems and networks as the law allows, in order to protect them and the data they hold. Do not expect privacy for personal material that you store on company systems.

When you leave the company, return all equipment within five working days of your last day. Remote employees receive a prepaid shipping box from IT.

## 22. Security and confidentiality

Every employee signs a confidentiality and invention assignment agreement before starting work. Customer data, source code, product plans, financial information, and personal information about employees are confidential. Share confidential information only with people who need it for their work, and only through company systems.

The IT and Security Policy defines the data classes and the rules for each. Customer meter data has the strictest class. Never copy it to a personal device or account.

Your obligations of confidentiality continue after you leave the company.

Keep your own records current. If your address, your legal name, your emergency contact, or your tax status changes, update PeopleHub within 30 days. The company keeps personnel records confidential and shares them only with those who need them for payroll, benefits, and legal compliance. You may ask People Operations to see your own personnel file.

## 23. Code of conduct

Act honestly, follow the law, and treat colleagues, customers, and partners with respect. Do not misuse company assets. Record business information accurately: time sheets, expense reports, test results, and sales records must be true and complete. If you see something that may break the law or this code, report it to your manager, to People Operations, to Legal, or through the ethics hotline in Section 34.

Alcohol may be served at company events. You are responsible for your behavior at those events. Do not work under the influence of alcohol or drugs. In the Boulder lab and on customer sites, this is a safety rule and a violation may lead to immediate removal from the site.

## 24. Anti-harassment and anti-retaliation

Cardinal Energy does not tolerate harassment, including sexual harassment, or bullying, by or toward any employee, contractor, customer, or visitor. Harassment includes unwelcome conduct, spoken, written, or physical, that is based on a protected characteristic and that creates a hostile or offensive work environment.

If you experience or witness harassment, report it to your manager, to any member of People Operations, or to Marcus Delgado, VP People. You may also use the ethics hotline. People Operations investigates every report promptly and keeps the matter as confidential as the investigation allows.

Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern in good faith, or who takes part in an investigation, is prohibited and is itself a violation of this policy.

All employees complete harassment prevention training within 60 days of joining and every two years after that. Managers complete an additional manager module.

## 25. Conflicts of interest and outside work

Avoid situations in which your personal interests conflict with the company's. Examples are holding a financial interest in a supplier or competitor, hiring or supervising a relative or partner, and using company information for personal gain. Disclose any possible conflict to Legal at legal@cardinal-energy.example. Disclosure usually resolves the matter.

You may do outside work, including paid work, if it does not compete with Cardinal Energy, does not use company time, equipment, or confidential information, and does not affect your performance. Get written approval from your manager and Legal before you join the board of a for-profit company or start work for any company in the energy sector.

Relatives and partners of employees may work at Cardinal Energy, but not in a position where one supervises the other or has influence over the other's pay, review, or promotion. If a personal relationship develops between two people in the same reporting line, both should tell People Operations, who will arrange a change in reporting in confidence.

If you contribute to open source software in your own time, you do not need approval, provided the project does not compete with our products and you do not use company code. Contributions made as part of your job need your manager's approval and follow the open source guidelines on Perch.

## 26. Gifts and entertainment

You may accept a gift from a vendor, customer, or partner only if its value is modest. The limit is $100 per vendor per calendar year. Decline or return anything above the limit and tell your manager. Cash and cash equivalents, such as gift cards, may never be accepted in any amount. Meals and entertainment offered by a vendor in the ordinary course of business are acceptable if a vendor representative is present and the occasion has a business purpose.

Many of our customers are public utilities and government bodies with strict gift rules of their own. Do not give anything of value to an employee of a public utility or a government official without approval from Legal.

## 27. Referral program

We pay a referral award of $4,000 when a candidate you refer is hired into a full-time role. The award is paid through payroll after the new hire completes 90 days of employment, provided you are still employed on that date. Submit the referral in PeopleHub before the candidate applies. Executives, members of the Recruiting team, and the hiring manager for the role are not eligible for the award. Andre Baptiste, Head of Recruiting, decides any dispute about who made a referral first.

## 28. Internal transfers

You may apply for an open role on another team after 12 months in your current role. Tell your manager before you interview. Internal candidates are interviewed by the hiring team, but the process is usually shorter than for external candidates. When an internal transfer is agreed, the two managers set a transfer date no more than six weeks after the offer is accepted.

## 29. Learning and development

Every full-time employee has a professional development allowance, described in the Benefits Summary. Your manager approves its use. The company also runs internal programs: an engineering tech talk every other Friday, a new-manager course twice a year, and a mentoring program that opens for sign-up each February.

Time spent on training that your manager has assigned is working time.

The mentoring program pairs you with a more experienced colleague from another team for six months. Pairs meet at least once a month. Engineering tech talks are on alternate Fridays at 11:00 Pacific and are open to everyone in the company. Recordings are kept on Perch.

New managers take the new-manager course in their first six months in the role. It covers one-on-ones, feedback, hiring, and the parts of this handbook that managers apply most often: time off, leave, and expenses.

## 30. Workplace safety

Report any injury, near miss, or unsafe condition to your manager and to Workplace at workplace@cardinal-energy.example on the day it happens. In an emergency, call 911 first.

The Boulder lab has additional rules. Anyone who enters the high-voltage test bay must have completed electrical safety training within the past 12 months and must wear the protective equipment posted at the bay door. No one works alone in the high-voltage test bay. Owen Castellanos, Director of Hardware Engineering, is the lab safety officer. Visitors to the lab must be escorted at all times.

Field Operations employees follow the customer's site safety rules in addition to ours. If the two differ, follow the stricter rule.

If you work at a desk, Workplace will assess your setup on request and provide an adjustable chair, a monitor arm, or a sit-stand desk where it is needed. Report pain or strain early. An injury that happens in the course of work is covered by workers' compensation insurance, and People Operations will help you file the claim.

Weapons are not allowed in any Cardinal office, lab, or vehicle, or at any company event. Threats and violence of any kind lead to immediate removal from the workplace.

## 31. Offices and facilities

Each office has an office guide on Perch with the address, opening hours, badge rules, parking, and visitor procedure. Wear your badge in the office. Register visitors in advance and stay with them while they are in the building. The offices are for work. Do not run a personal business from the office or receive personal packages there on a regular basis.

## 32. Communication norms

Use company chat for quick questions and email for anything that needs a record or that goes outside the company. Put decisions in writing in a place the whole team can find. Default to public channels over direct messages for work topics. Do not expect a reply outside the other person's working hours unless you are paging the on-call engineer through PagerLine.

The all-hands meeting is on the first Thursday of each month at 11:00 Pacific. It is recorded for people who cannot attend.

Every meeting has an owner, an agenda sent in advance, and written notes with decisions and action items. If a meeting has no agenda, you may decline it. Keep recurring meetings to 25 or 50 minutes so that people can move between rooms. Friday afternoons are kept free of recurring meetings.

Write so that a colleague in another office and another time zone can act on what you wrote without asking a follow-up question. State what you need, from whom, and by when.

## 33. Media, social media, and public speaking

Only designated spokespeople speak for the company. If a member of the media or an industry analyst contacts you, do not answer their questions. Forward the inquiry to Callum Reid, Head of Marketing, at press@cardinal-energy.example.

You may say on social media that you work at Cardinal Energy. Do not share confidential information, customer names that have not been announced, or photographs of the Boulder lab. Make clear that your opinions are your own.

If you want to speak at a conference or publish an article about your work, ask your manager and Marketing at least four weeks ahead. Legal reviews any talk that covers unreleased products.

## 34. Raising concerns and the ethics hotline

We want to hear about problems early. You can raise a concern with your manager, your manager's manager, People Operations, or Legal. If you prefer to report anonymously, call the ethics hotline at 1-800-555-0142. An independent provider operates the hotline 24 hours a day and passes reports to the General Counsel, Fiona MacLeod. Reports about the General Counsel go to the CEO.

## 35. Leaving Cardinal Energy

If you decide to resign, we ask for at least two weeks of written notice to your manager. People Operations will schedule an exit interview and send you a checklist for returning equipment and transferring your work.

Your final pay includes salary through your last day and any accrued, unused PTO. Sick leave, floating holidays, volunteer time, and sabbatical time are not paid out. Vested stock options can be exercised for the period stated in your grant agreement. Health coverage continues through the last day of the month in which you leave, and you will receive information about continuing coverage at your own cost.

Cardinal Energy confirms dates of employment and job title to prospective employers. Send reference requests to People Operations.

## 36. Changes to this handbook

People Operations reviews this handbook once a year. Between versions, the company changes policies by posting announcements and memos on Perch. An announcement or memo posted after the effective date of this handbook takes precedence over the handbook on the topic it covers. A detailed policy document takes precedence over the handbook's summary of it. When two documents disagree, the one with the later effective date governs. If you are not sure which rule applies, ask People Operations.
