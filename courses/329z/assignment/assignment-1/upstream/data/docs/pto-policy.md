# Paid Time Off Policy

Policy number: HR-101
Effective: 2025-01-01
Owner: People Operations
Contact: Imani Brooks, Benefits Specialist (people@cardinal-energy.example)

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy describes how full-time and part-time employees of Cardinal Energy in the United States earn, request, and use paid time off (PTO). Rotterdam employees follow the Netherlands addendum. Sick leave, company holidays, and other kinds of leave are summarized in Section 8 so that the differences are clear.

## 2. Accrual

PTO accrues each semi-monthly pay period, starting on your first day. The annual rate depends on your length of service.

| Length of service | PTO per year |
|---|---|
| Less than 2 years | 18 days |
| 2 years to less than 5 years | 22 days |
| 5 years or more | 25 days |

The rate changes in the pay period that contains your service anniversary. Part-time employees accrue in proportion to their scheduled hours. PTO does not accrue during unpaid leave of more than 30 days.

Your balance is shown in PeopleHub in hours. One day is 8 hours for a full-time employee.

## 3. Requesting time off

Submit every request in PeopleHub. Your manager approves or declines it there.

- One or two days: request as early as you can. There is no minimum notice.
- Three or more consecutive working days: request at least two weeks ahead.
- More than 10 consecutive working days: the request also needs approval from your department's vice president.

Managers approve requests unless the absence would leave a team without required coverage, for example an on-call rotation with no available engineer. If two requests conflict, the earlier request has priority. A manager who declines a request must propose other dates.

## 4. Using time off

Take PTO in increments of at least 2 hours. Put your absence on your calendar and set an out-of-office reply. You are not expected to check email or chat while on PTO. If your team has an on-call rotation, arrange a swap before you leave.

A company holiday that falls inside a PTO period is not deducted from your balance. If you fall ill during PTO, you may convert the days of illness to sick leave by telling People Operations when you return.

## 5. Carryover

PTO is meant to be used in the year in which it is earned. At the end of each calendar year, you may carry over up to 5 days (40 hours) of unused PTO into the next year. Carried-over PTO must be used by March 31 of that year. Unused PTO above the carryover limit is forfeited on December 31, except where state law does not permit forfeiture. People Operations sends a balance reminder to every employee on October 1.

## 6. Negative balances

With your manager's approval you may take PTO before you have accrued it, down to a negative balance of 40 hours. If you leave the company with a negative balance, the amount is deducted from your final pay where the law allows.

## 7. Separation

When employment ends, accrued and unused PTO is paid out in your final pay at your base rate. PTO may not be used to extend your last day beyond your final working day.

## 8. Other paid leave

These kinds of leave are separate from PTO and have their own balances.

- Sick leave: 10 days per calendar year. It does not carry over.
- Company holidays: 11 per year, listed in the Employee Handbook.
- Floating holidays: 2 per calendar year. They do not carry over.
- Winter closure: the offices close from December 24 through January 1. The closure days that are not company holidays are paid and are not deducted from PTO.
- Bereavement, jury duty, volunteer time, and sabbatical: see the Employee Handbook.
- Parental leave: see the Parental Leave Policy.

## 9. Questions

Contact People Operations through the People queue in the HelpDesk portal. Questions about a balance shown in PeopleHub are usually answered within two working days.
