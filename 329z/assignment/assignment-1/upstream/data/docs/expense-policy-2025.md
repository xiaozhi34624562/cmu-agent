# Travel and Expense Policy (2025)

Policy number: FIN-201, version 2025.1
Effective: 2025-02-01
Owner: Finance and Accounting (Peter Andersson, Controller)
Approved by: Helen Okafor, CFO

## 1. Purpose and principles

Cardinal Energy pays for the reasonable costs of doing company business. This policy says what is reimbursable, what the limits are, and how to get repaid. Spend company money as carefully as you would spend your own. If an expense is not covered here, ask your manager before you incur it.

The policy applies to all employees. Contractors are reimbursed only as their contract provides.

## 2. Booking travel

Book flights, hotels, and rental cars through TripDesk. TripDesk applies our negotiated rates and lets us find you in an emergency. A booking made outside TripDesk is reimbursed only if TripDesk could not make the booking or if the outside price was lower, and you must attach a screenshot of the comparison.

Book air travel at least 14 days before departure. Fares rise sharply inside two weeks.

## 3. Air travel

Fly economy class. For a flight with a scheduled duration of more than 6 hours, you may book premium economy. Business class is not reimbursable unless the CFO approves it for a documented medical reason. Seat selection fees and one checked bag are reimbursable. Airline club memberships and in-flight purchases other than Wi-Fi used for work are not.

You keep the frequent flyer miles you earn. Do not choose a more expensive flight to earn miles.

## 4. Lodging

The limit for a hotel room is $220 per night before taxes. In the high-cost cities listed here, the limit is $300 per night: New York, San Francisco, Boston, Washington DC, and London. If a conference hotel is over the limit, the conference rate is acceptable when you attach the conference booking page.

Staying with friends or family is allowed. You may expense a gift for your host of up to $50 per stay in place of lodging.

## 5. Meals while traveling

When you travel overnight on company business, meals are reimbursed up to a daily limit of $60 per day for domestic travel and $80 per day for international travel. The limit covers breakfast, lunch, dinner, and tips. It is a limit and not an allowance: you are reimbursed for what you spend, up to the limit, with receipts. On the day you depart and the day you return, the limit is half of the daily amount.

Alcohol with a meal is reimbursable within the daily limit, up to two drinks.

## 6. Ground transportation

Use public transit, a rideshare service, or a taxi, whichever is reasonable. Rent a car only when it costs less than the alternatives or when the destination requires one. Rent a compact or midsize car. Decline the rental company's insurance in the United States, because the company's policy covers you.

If you drive your own car for business, mileage is reimbursed at $0.67 per mile. Commuting between your home and your assigned office is not business travel. Parking and tolls on business trips are reimbursable. Fines are not.

## 7. Customer entertainment

Meals and events with customers or prospects are reimbursable up to $90 per person, counting every attendee including Cardinal employees. Get your manager's approval before the event. The expense report must list every attendee and the business purpose. Remember that many of our customers are public utilities with gift rules. See the Gifts and entertainment section of the Employee Handbook.

## 8. Team events

Managers may spend up to $40 per person per quarter on team meals and events. Unused amounts do not roll over to the next quarter.

## 9. Other reimbursable expenses

- Work-related books and subscriptions approved by your manager.
- Shipping of company equipment.
- Visa and passport fees needed for a business trip.
- Mobile roaming charges on a business trip, when you have used the roaming plan IT recommends.

Software, hardware, and anything that needs a contract must go through Procurement and must not be bought on an expense report.

## 10. Not reimbursable

Personal travel added to a business trip, travel costs for companions, clothing, personal grooming, fitness center fees at hotels, minibar purchases, in-room films, traffic and parking fines, and loss of personal property.

## 11. Approval

Your manager approves every expense report. In addition:

- Any single expense over $500 requires approval from your department's vice president.
- Any single expense over $5,000 requires approval from the CFO.

An approver may not approve their own expenses or those of their own manager.

## 12. Submitting expenses

Submit expenses in Ledgerly within 30 days of the date the expense was incurred. Attach an itemized receipt for every expense over $25. A card statement is not a receipt. If you lose a receipt, complete the missing receipt form in Ledgerly. More than three missing receipt forms in a quarter will be raised with your manager.

Approved expenses are reimbursed by direct deposit within 10 business days of final approval.

## 13. Corporate cards

Employees who travel more than four times a year may request a corporate card through Ledgerly. Card charges still need an expense report with receipts. Personal charges on a corporate card are not allowed. Report a lost card to the card provider and to Accounts Payable on the same day.

## 14. Questions and exceptions

Ask Accounts Payable at ap@cardinal-energy.example. Nora Lindgren, Accounts Payable Specialist, handles most questions. Exceptions to this policy need written approval from the Controller before the expense is incurred.
